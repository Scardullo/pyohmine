Yes. A minimal NixOS ISO is perfectly fine for this, and for the setup you're describing, I'd actually recommend it.

The ISO only needs to give you enough of an environment to:

Boot the machine.
Partition/format the disk.
Mount the filesystems.
Get networking working.
Install NixOS.
Bring in your existing flake/configuration.

Your actual desktop environment, packages, Hyprland, Waybar, Zsh, etc. don't need to be on the ISO. 
They're defined by your configuration.nix.

Your hypothetical reinstall

You could do:

NixOS minimal ISO
       ↓
Partition + format disk
       ↓
Mount /mnt
       ↓
nixos-generate-config --root /mnt
       ↓
Put your configuration.nix + flake.nix + flake.lock
       ↓
nixos-install --flake /mnt/etc/nixos#nixos
       ↓
Reboot
       ↓
Your configured NixOS

One correction to what we discussed earlier: during an actual fresh install, 
you generally want to generate the hardware configuration against the mounted target system:

sudo nixos-generate-config --root /mnt

That creates:

/mnt/etc/nixos/configuration.nix
/mnt/etc/nixos/hardware-configuration.nix

Then you can put your GitHub versions of flake.nix, flake.lock, and configuration.nix into /mnt/etc/nixos/, 
while keeping the newly generated hardware-configuration.nix.

Then install directly from the flake:

sudo nixos-install --flake /mnt/etc/nixos#nixos

So yes: minimal ISO → your flake → your configuration → essentially the same declared system.

And because your flake.lock is in GitHub, you don't need to run nix flake update during the reinstall. 
That would be a separate decision to update your locked packages.





/mnt/etc/nixos is primarily because you're doing this from the installer environment before the new system exists.

The important thing is understanding what /mnt represents.

While you're in the installer ISO

You boot the minimal ISO, and your new SSD might be mounted at:

/mnt

So the filesystem you're building looks like:

Installer environment
        │
        └── /mnt  ← your NEW NixOS filesystem
               │
               ├── etc/
               │    └── nixos/
               │         ├── configuration.nix
               │         ├── hardware-configuration.nix
               │         ├── flake.nix
               │         └── flake.lock
               │
               ├── home/
               ├── usr/
               └── ...

Therefore, from the installer's perspective, the future system's:

/etc/nixos

is currently:

/mnt/etc/nixos

That's why you use:

sudo nixos-generate-config --root /mnt

It generates the configuration inside the new system:

/mnt/etc/nixos/
After you reboot

The new filesystem becomes / instead of /mnt.

So:

/mnt/etc/nixos

becomes:

/etc/nixos

Your normal running system sees:

/etc/nixos/
├── configuration.nix
├── hardware-configuration.nix
├── flake.nix
└── flake.lock

So the transition is:

         INSTALLER ISO
              
/mnt ────────────────────→ becomes /
 │                            │
 └── etc/nixos                └── etc/nixos
